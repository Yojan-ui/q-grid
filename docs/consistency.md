# Consistency notes

_Generated by `scripts/write_consistency.py` from `results/bench_v3/daily.csv` (one row per method, dataset and day, written by `scripts/write_bench_v3.py` from `runs.csv`) and `config.yaml`. Each section is a check that the script asserts; if one failed, this file would not be written._

## 1. The exact master never loses to a QAOA master

In the earlier hybrid-Benders design the QAOA-master variant beat the exact-master variant on some days. Cause: the QAOA proposal was used unverified while the exact master was restricted to a trust region, so the two runs took different paths through the day and either could end up cheaper; the difference was path dependence, not a better quantum answer.

Fix (`qgrid/v2/mpc.py`, `V2Config.verify_fallback`, test `test_exact_master_never_loses_to_a_heuristic_master`): every heuristic master proposal is checked against the exact master on the same cuts and trust region, and the exact proposal is the one used — the tiered fallback of CLAUDE.md. The heuristic's agreement rate is recorded. Result on the RVCE evaluation days of this run:

| Design variant | days | same cost as exact master | days it beat the exact master | heuristic proposal = exact proposal |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| QAOA master | 30 | 30 | 0 | 99.0 % |
| QIEA master | 30 | 30 | 0 | 96.3 % |

## 2. Gap and critical downtime come from the same run

Every figure of a method-day — gap to the best plan, certified gap, critical downtime, unserved energy, bill — is computed from one row of `runs.csv` (one simulated day of one method). The scorecard averages those rows; it never combines a gap from one run with a downtime from another.

Q-Grid on RVCE (30 days): gap 0.76 %, critical downtime 0.14 h/day (any shortfall), 0.00 h/day counting only slots with more than 1 % of critical load unserved, critical energy unserved 0.059 kWh/day.
- Strict downtime counts every 15-min slot with *any* unserved critical load (> 1e-6 kW), however small. A small gap next to a non-zero strict downtime means the shortfall is tiny in energy; the >1 % measure and the unserved energy show its size.
- At the critical value of lost load (Rs 1000/kWh) Q-Grid's mean critical shortfall is worth Rs 59 per day.

## 3. Value of lost load (VoLL), and gaps with and without it

- VoLL inside every method's objective: non-critical load Rs 40/kWh, critical load Rs 1000/kWh.
- Source: **ASSUMPTION** (`config.yaml` → `outage_costs`). No official Indian or BESCOM VoLL figure has been sourced; it is a modelling choice that makes shedding load never cheaper than serving it. The same values are used by every method on every day.
- With VoLL = the objective the optimisers minimise (primary). Money only = energy + diesel + terminal value without VoLL; a method can look better on money alone by shedding more load.

## 4. Reference plan and certified bounds

- The best plan for a day is the cheapest feasible plan known: the central MILP with perfect information, or a controller's realised plan where it is cheaper within the MILP's tolerance (relative gap 1e-4). No gap is negative.
- Certified gap uses the best proven lower bound (LP relaxation, MILP dual bound, Lagrangian bound with ADMM prices). A valid bound can never exceed a feasible plan; on 0 of 150 dataset-days the computed bound did (solver tolerance or a time-limited solve) and was capped at the best plan.

## 5. Battery wear is priced the same way everywhere

- Every optimiser (Q-Grid, per-building and central MPC, GA, PSO, the best possible plan, the certified bounds) prices battery wear at Rs 4.17 per kWh charged or discharged = battery cost / (2 × cycle life × depth of discharge) (`config.yaml` → `economics`, `battery_wear`; ASSUMPTIONS). The scored objective includes the same term, and the scorecard's degradation-cost column uses the same rate.
- The earlier design rows (hybrid Benders, first prototype) predate this and do not price wear; they are design history under Verify, not scorecard rows.
