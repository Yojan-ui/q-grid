# Consistency notes

_Generated by `scripts/write_consistency.py` from `results/v2/benchmark_v2.csv` (the earlier hybrid-Benders benchmark, computed on the previous template load profiles) and `config.yaml`._

## 1. Why the QAOA-master variant beat the exact-master variant on the outage day

On the outage day the Benders variant with a QAOA master landed 0.25 % from the optimum and the variant with an exact master 0.36 % (a difference of Rs 372 on Rs 339,114). Over the six named days the QAOA master was better on 1, worse on 5 and tied on 0.

Cause — 'exact master' did not mean an exact decision:
- Each hour the master MILP was solved to optimality (HiGHS, relative gap 1e-6), but the Benders loop stopped at an iteration cap (6) with a bound gap still open: 1.22 % (exact master) and 1.90 % (QAOA master) on average on that day. The decision taken was the best plan found so far, not a proven optimum.
- Both variants also share consensus ADMM stopped at a 1 kW tolerance and a trust region on the master.
- The two variants therefore follow different, path-dependent trajectories through the day; a difference of 0.11 % is well inside the open Benders gap. It is noise of the heuristic, not evidence that QAOA finds better plans.
- Resolution in the current design: the Benders loop is gone. Each building's commitment is solved exactly by branch and bound at the agreed prices, so 'exact' means exact for that sub-problem, and the remaining gap is measured and certified against proven lower bounds (docs/math.md).

## 2. A 0.2 % gap next to 1.75 h of critical downtime

Both numbers come from the same run (QAOA-master variant, outage day): gap 0.25 %, critical downtime 1.75 h, critical energy unserved 0.081 kWh (optimum: 0.00 h, 0.000 kWh).
- Critical downtime counts every 15-min slot in which *any* critical load is unserved, however small (> 1e-6 kW). Here the shortfall over the whole day was 0.081 kWh, spread over 7 slots — numerical crumbs of a solver tolerance, not a real blackout of a critical load.
- At the value of lost load used for critical load (Rs 1000/kWh) that shortfall is worth about Rs 81, which is why the gap barely moves.
- From benchmark v3 on, both measures are reported side by side: strict downtime (any shortfall) and downtime counting only slots where more than 1 % of the critical load is unserved, plus the unserved energy itself.

## 3. Value of lost load (VoLL) and gaps with and without it

- VoLL used inside the objective: non-critical load Rs 40/kWh, critical load Rs 1000/kWh.
- Source: **ASSUMPTION** (config.yaml `outage_costs`). No official Indian or BESCOM VoLL figure has been sourced; it is a modelling choice that makes shedding load never 'cheaper' than serving it.
- Every gap is reported both ways. With VoLL = objective including the value of lost load (what the optimisers minimise). Without VoLL = money only (energy + diesel + demand charge + terminal value); a method can look better on money alone by shedding more load, which is why the VoLL version is the primary one.

| Design (earlier benchmark, six named days) | gap with VoLL % | gap without VoLL (money only) % |
|---|---:|---:|
| Earlier prototype (1-hour horizon, binary coordination) | 11.68 | 10.44 |
| Hybrid Benders, exact master | 1.84 | 1.89 |
| Hybrid Benders, QAOA master | 2.39 | 2.41 |
| Hybrid Benders, QIEA master (quantum-inspired, classical) | 2.96 | 3.04 |
