## Central case (mid-point assumptions)

- Hardware for 9 building nodes: Rs 7.9 lakh; maintenance Rs 0.8 lakh per year.
- Bill saving vs today: Rs -5.8 lakh per year (mean of 30 RVCE days × 365).
- Extra battery wear from cycling the existing batteries: Rs 8.9 lakh per year (586 kWh more throughput per day × Rs 4.17/kWh).
- Net value per year: Rs -15.5 lakh.
- 10-year NPV at 8 %: Rs -51.9 lakh before battery wear, Rs -111.6 lakh after.
- Payback: not reached before battery wear, not reached after battery wear.
- Break-even battery wear: Rs -3.62 per kWh of throughput (the central assumption is Rs 4.17).

## What this means

- Every optimiser in the scorecard prices battery wear in its objective (`config.yaml` → `battery_wear`), so the batteries cycle only when a price spread beats the wear. An earlier run without that term cycled the batteries for small spreads and lost money after wear (`results/impact/wear_pilot_2.96.csv` documents the pilot that led to the change).
- Reliability (critical downtime) and resilience to a network failure are benefits not priced here.

## Scale path

- **RVCE pilot**: 9 buildings, one node each (this page).
- **Bengaluru campuses**: the same node per building; no campus-wide server (the benchmark's four other campuses).
  - RVCE, Bengaluru (case study): gap to the best plan 8.4 %, bill change -0.9 % (HT-2(c)(ii)), 9.1 s per decision
  - BDG2 university site 'Fox' (Arizona): gap to the best plan 9.9 %, bill change +0.1 % (HT-2(c)(ii)), 2.7 s per decision
  - BDG2 university site 'Bull' (Texas): gap to the best plan 9.8 %, bill change +0.3 % (HT-2(c)(ii)), 2.8 s per decision
  - BDG2 university site 'Cockatoo' (New York): gap to the best plan 11.8 %, bill change +1.4 % (HT-2(c)(ii)), 2.3 s per decision
  - CU-BEMS office building, Bangkok (7 floors): gap to the best plan 27.7 %, bill change +5.5 % (HT-2(c)(ii)), 4.0 s per decision
- **Feeders**: campuses and buildings on one distribution feeder agree the same way, with neighbours only (per-decision work and messages depend on a building's neighbours, not on the size of the network (consensus ADMM); not yet tested on a feeder model).
